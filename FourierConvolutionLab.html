WHAT TO NOTICE

Convolution adds supports.

What is exact here, and what is numerical?

Exact formulas: the transform cards use the continuous-time convention printed at the top. Sinusoids and impulses are interpreted as tempered distributions.

Plots: ideal δ impulses are drawn as vertical stems whose signed height is the impulse coefficient—the integral of that impulse. Convolution with an ordinary function is evaluated exactly as a sum of shifted, scaled copies. Narrow unit-area pulses are used only inside the numerical Fourier comparison. Ideal sinusoids are truncated by the lab window, so their plotted spectra show leakage rather than literal delta lines.

Important boundary: products such as δ(t)² are not defined in ordinary distribution theory. When both chosen signals contain impulses, multiplication mode is only a finite-width visualization and the app says so.
